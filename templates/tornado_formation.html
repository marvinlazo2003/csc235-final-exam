How Tornadoes Form

Tornadoes are violently rotating columns of air that extend from thunderstorms to the ground.

Step 1: Thunderstorm Development

Tornadoes begin inside powerful thunderstorms called supercells. These storms form when warm, moist air meets cold, dry air in the atmosphere.

Step 2: Wind Shear

Wind shear (changes in wind speed and direction with height) causes the storm to rotate horizontally.

Step 3: Updraft Tilts Rotation

A strong updraft of rising air tilts the rotating air vertically, forming a mesocyclone.

Step 4: Tornado Formation

When the rotating column tightens and extends downward from the cloud, a tornado forms.

Step 5: Ground Contact

Once the funnel cloud touches the ground, it officially becomes a tornado.

Key Conditions Needed

  • Warm, moist air near the ground
  • Cold, dry air above
  • Strong wind shear
  • Unstable atmosphere
